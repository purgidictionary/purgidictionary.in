Documentation journey

From immersion to a digital resource

The dictionary emerged from community engagement, language learning and field documentation in Kargil.

1979

K. Rangan — Purki Grammar

An important Indian academic contribution to grammatical documentation of Purki/Purgi.

2007

PurigSkad — Pakistan

A grammatical collection from Pakistan, rather than a dictionary. It is acknowledged here as part of the wider history of Purgi linguistic documentation.

2018

Marius Zemp — A Grammar of Purik Tibetan

A major modern scholarly contribution to linguistic documentation of Purik/Purgi.

2021

Dr. Winsy Abraham relocates to Kargil

After more than 17 years in the corporate sector, Dr. Abraham relocated permanently from Indore to Kargil with his family in October 2021, choosing to devote his time to community development and social service.

2022–2025

Community immersion and field documentation

Between 2022 and 2025, he undertook sustained community engagement, travelling approximately 500 km and visiting more than 60 villages to document vocabulary and observe local usage.

3 June 2025

Purgi enters Ladakh's official-language framework

The Ladakh Official Languages Regulation comes into force and includes Purgi among the official languages of the Union Territory.

6 June 2026

Purgi script publicly launched in Kargil

A major opportunity to build the next generation of the dictionary around both Purgi script and Latin transliteration.

July 2026

First edition of the Purgi Dictionary published

The 5,000-entry multilingual digital edition brings together Purgi, English, Hindi and Urdu and is freely available through Android and iOS applications.

Phase II

Purgi Living Dictionary

Proposed continuation: script integration, Latin transliteration, community verification, expanded vocabulary, idioms and proverbs, learning resources and audio where feasible.
