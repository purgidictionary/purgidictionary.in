The language

About Purgi

Purgi (also known as Purik, Purki or Purigskad) is a major local language of the Kargil region of Ladakh, with a living oral, cultural and literary tradition.

A major local language of Kargil

Purgi is spoken across Kargil and the wider historic Purig region of Ladakh. Kargil lies at the centre of the historic Purig region, and Purgi continues to play an important role in everyday communication and cultural life. It is closely related to Ladakhi and Balti and is classified within the Tibetic branch of the Sino-Tibetan language family. Purgi-speaking communities are also found in areas of Baltistan.

Why Kargil matters

Kargil is a high-altitude, mountainous cold-desert region with severe winters, difficult terrain and a short agricultural season. At the same time, the district has historically connected communities and routes across the wider Himalayan region. These conditions have shaped a culture in which language, memory and community knowledge have remained deeply important.

A new written and digital moment

Purgi was included among the official languages of the Union Territory of Ladakh in the Ladakh Official Languages Regulation, 2025. Government Degree College Kargil also introduced conversational Ability Enhancement Courses in Purgi and other regional languages in 2025. The Purgi script was publicly launched in Kargil on 6 June 2026. Together, these developments create an important opportunity to support the written, educational and digital future of the language.

Accessibility and script

Although Purgi is the mother tongue of many people in the region, written literacy in the language remains a challenge for many speakers. This makes accessible documentation and practical learning resources especially important.

The first edition uses Latin-script forms for Purgi words to make the vocabulary accessible to readers who may not yet be familiar with the Purgi script. This is an accessibility measure, not a replacement for Purgi writing. The proposed next phase aims to integrate the Purgi script alongside Latin transliteration.

Latin transliteration for accessibility; Purgi script for the written future of the language.

Why documentation matters

Purgi has a long linguistic history in the Purig region. Earlier linguistic and vocabulary works exist, but a freely accessible multilingual digital dictionary bringing together Purgi, English, Hindi and Urdu was still needed to support learning, teaching and research.

Much of a language's vocabulary, expressions and cultural knowledge is carried through speech, memory, oral traditions and everyday community life. Documentation helps make this knowledge more accessible to learners, researchers and future generations.

Documentation today is therefore about more than recording words. It is about creating a durable resource that can support learning, teaching, research, translation, creative work, cultural preservation and the transmission of Purgi to future generations.

A long-lived language needs a durable digital record.
The dictionary brings 5,000 Purgi lexical entries together with English, Hindi and Urdu meanings and makes the resource freely accessible through mobile applications.
